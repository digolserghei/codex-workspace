03Causes and diagnosis

Infertility
factors

01

Female age

As a woman ages, both the number and quality of her eggs gradually decline, reducing the likelihood of natural conception. The decline in fertility becomes more pronounced after 35 and accelerates considerably after 40. At the same time, the risk of chromosomal abnormalities in the embryo and pregnancy loss increases.

02

Ovulation disorders

Absent or irregular ovulation prevents natural conception. Common causes include polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), thyroid dysfunction, hyperprolactinaemia and other hormonal disorders. Many of these conditions can be diagnosed and treated.

03

Fallopian tube disorders

Blocked or damaged fallopian tubes prevent the egg and sperm from meeting. The main causes include previous pelvic inflammatory disease, sexually transmitted infections and the effects of surgery.

04

Endometriosis

Endometriosis can cause chronic inflammation, adhesions and changes to pelvic anatomy. Some forms of the condition reduce the likelihood of natural conception, particularly when the ovaries and fallopian tubes are affected. The impact depends on the extent and location of the disease.

05

Male factor

Changes in sperm count, motility or morphology can significantly reduce the likelihood of fertilisation. Causes include varicocele, hormonal and genetic disorders, infections and other conditions. An infertility assessment should therefore include both partners.

ART

Assisted reproductive technology (ART) comprises medical technologies and diagnostic and treatment methods aimed at achieving pregnancy when some or all stages of conception take place outside the woman’s body.

06In vitro fertilisation

IVF

Method and possibilities

IVF is a modern infertility treatment in which an egg is fertilised outside the woman’s body, in a laboratory.

The resulting embryos are transferred into the uterine cavity for further development. IVF is used when natural conception is difficult or impossible.

The method helps overcome many causes of infertility and significantly increases the likelihood of achieving a long-awaited pregnancy and the birth of a healthy child.

07The path to pregnancy

IVF
stages

01

Preparing for the programme

Before IVF begins, the doctor assesses both partners’ reproductive health and determines the most appropriate treatment strategy. Investigations may include hormone tests, pelvic ultrasound, semen analysis and other tests as indicated. The results are used to develop an individual programme plan.

02

Stimulation and egg retrieval

Hormonal stimulation makes it possible to obtain several eggs in one cycle. Follicle growth is monitored by ultrasound and, when necessary, blood tests. When the follicles reach the required stage of development, the eggs are retrieved by ovarian puncture, usually under sedation.

03

Fertilisation and embryo development

The retrieved eggs are fertilised in the embryology laboratory using conventional IVF or ICSI. Over the following days, specialists monitor embryo development and assess embryo quality. Preimplantation genetic testing may be performed when indicated.

04

Embryo transfer

The selected embryo is transferred into the uterine cavity using a thin catheter, usually under ultrasound guidance. Transfer may take place in the current cycle or a later cycle after embryo cryopreservation. A blood hCG test is performed 9–14 days after transfer to determine whether pregnancy has occurred.
